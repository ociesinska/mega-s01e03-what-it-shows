What needs fixing or investigating

Parser: respect the input’s date order

The shared parser reads dates month first. A day-first tenant’s app or email input can therefore swap day and month. For example, R-41200 reports 7 April becoming 4 July, and R-41203 reports 2 December becoming 12 February.

Confirm the input contract for each channel and parse according to that contract; reject invalid or ambiguous input rather than silently swapping it. Verify both ambiguous dates and days greater than twelve. Tenant locale comes from the directory, not the reporter’s browser.

Form hint: match the tenant’s date order

For day-first tenants using booking-v3, the form prints MM/DD/YYYY, while the form reads dates in the tenant’s order. A person following the hint gets a swapped date. R-41230 is one example: copper.studio, booking-v3, form channel, 6 January versus 1 June. Intake tagged that report parser, demonstrating why intake tags do not determine the split.

Align the hint with the form’s parsing contract and test the actual displayed field and submitted value together. Fixing the shared parser alone does not correct misleading instructions.

Timing: investigate without assuming a root cause

The evidence table preserves all five reports. One-day shifts and different days in Lisbon and Toronto could involve time zones or date-only conversions. An invite arriving before its booking could instead involve scheduling or delivery order. These are hypotheses, not findings.

Obtain event and booking IDs, creation and send timestamps with offsets, tenant and recipient time zones, original invite payloads, calendar display settings, and delivery logs. Reproduce each symptom, including June-only behavior. Split this provisional investigation further if the evidence establishes different mechanisms. Do not claim a timezone fix has been proven.

Proposed triage rule

The accepted decision 0001 and triage prose date from 2024. They match subjects without reading bodies. Preserve that accepted record as history; this page proposes a replacement for desk approval:

  1. Read the body and establish the observed versus expected behavior, tenant, locale, channel, and form version. Ask for missing context.
  2. Use a matching subject only to find candidates. Merge after verifying the same failure mechanism; different subjects can still describe the same mechanism.
  3. Keep distinct mechanisms separate, even with identical subjects. With unknown tenant or contradictory evidence, keep the report unclassified and investigate rather than force a merge.
  4. Count unique report IDs once. Preserve provenance and link related investigations.
  5. Re-read the rendered views and reconcile the live queue before publishing current totals.
